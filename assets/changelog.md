v1.6.0
Subtitles
* Fixed subtitles not turning on by themselves. For many titles the search came back empty in every language, so nothing was shown even though subtitles existed. The search now finds them again, and a subtitle in your language is switched on as soon as it is found.
* The automatic pick is much better at choosing the right file. It now looks at which release a subtitle was made for, prefers one that matches the stream you are watching, and checks the frame rate, because a file made for a different frame rate slides further out of step the longer a title runs.
* Subtitles made from a cinema recording are avoided whenever a proper one exists. They belong to a different cut of the film and rarely line up.
* Subtitles written for deaf and hard of hearing viewers, the ones that describe door slams and music, are no longer picked by default. They are still used when they are the only ones available.
* Automatic sync no longer moves a subtitle that is already well timed. It used to read a correctly timed file as about half a second late, because speech is heard a beat after a line is meant to appear, and then "corrected" it. Shifts under roughly a second are now left to the Sync button.
* Automatic sync refuses a correction that is too large to be one. A subtitle written for a different version of an episode can be half a minute out in the middle and a second out at the start, and no single shift repairs that: it only changes which half is wrong. Measurements past fifteen seconds are now read as evidence that the file does not belong to this version, and the subtitle is left where it is instead of being dragged.
* Automatic sync reaches its answer sooner. It listens in half minute stretches, and the first stretch was being thrown away before it could be judged, however much speech it held, so the earliest anything could move was about a minute and a half into a title. A first stretch carrying plenty of dialogue is now weighed straight away, roughly forty seconds in. Quiet openings still wait for a second stretch, because one is not enough to tell a real offset from a coincidence.
* Automatic sync says once that it has started listening, for a second, so a subtitle shifting a minute later reads as the setting doing its job rather than as a glitch. It used to say it at the start of every attempt, and an attempt begins again on a new subtitle, on a change of server and after each time the dialogue could not be matched, so one film could carry the same line several times over, usually followed by nothing at all. Once a sitting now. The message saying by how much the timing moved is unchanged.
* Switching server no longer throws the subtitle out of step, and a subtitle that was thrown out by the old behaviour is repaired. The stretched timings had been saved as a learned correction and were being reapplied on every later play, so the film stayed wrong even after the cause was fixed. Those saved corrections are retired; the first play of a title measures afresh. When the new stream had a different length, the timings were stretched to match it, on the assumption that a different length means a different speed. It usually does not: a server that stops short in the credits is the same film at the same speed, and stretching a subtitle by that difference put every line minutes out of place. The subtitle you had is now kept exactly as it is, and a better fitting one is looked for in the background, which is what the app had been promising itself and never actually doing.
* Find subtitles online opens on your own subtitle language, and its language list is the searchable one used everywhere else, with the current choice ticked and in view.

Player
* Playback on devices with little memory, such as streaming sticks and older boxes, now uses a buffer sized to the memory the device really has. The old buffer could use nearly all of it on a high quality stream, which showed up as a picture that froze, a device that became sluggish, or the app closing back to the home screen. Phones and devices with 3 GB of memory or more behave exactly as before.
* Sources that offer 4K play in 4K. Some players build their quality list in the app's memory rather than fetching it, so the app only ever saw the single quality their own player happened to start on, and a title available in 4K played at 1080p or lower. The whole list is picked up now, so quality follows your connection up to whatever the source really has.
* Some sources played every title at a low quality. They hand out two quality lists at almost the same moment, one holding a single low quality and one holding the full range, and whichever arrived first was the one used. The app now looks at what it has been handed and waits a moment for the better list before starting. The same check keeps a source from stalling on a list that is no longer there, which is why one of them sometimes did not start at all.
* Fixed a source that now and then gave up before it started, most often one offering 4K. Its own player kept running in the background after the stream had been handed over, so the same film was being fetched twice at once and the picture never arrived. It is stopped now the moment the stream is taken, which also saves the data that second copy was using.
* Sources in other audio languages, such as Hindi and Russian, appear within a couple of seconds instead of up to a minute. They are listed as they are found rather than after every source has answered.
* Fixed some high resolution streams that stayed on 0:00 and never started, and a network hiccup while loading one that ended playback instead of being retried.
* Fixed the quality shown for widescreen films. A film shot wider than the screen is letterboxed before it is encoded, so its 1080p version is 1920 by 808 and its 4K version is 3840 by 1636. The app read only the height and called those 720p and 1440p, so most films looked a step worse than they were. Nothing about what plays has changed, only what it is called.
* Subtitles that arrived as Ã© where an é belongs are repaired. Some files are already damaged when we get them: somebody's tool read them in the wrong alphabet and saved the result, so "één" is spelled out as "Ã©Ã©n" in the file itself. The app now recognises that and undoes it, and leaves a correctly written file alone.
* A double tap no longer disturbs the controls at all. Skipping ten seconds at the edge of the picture used to leave the bar sitting over the film afterwards, and pausing with a double tap made it blink away and back. The bar now waits a fifth of a second before it moves, which is shorter than the gap between anybody's two taps, so a double tap cancels it before anything has happened and the bar never moves.
* The control bar no longer reappears by itself in the middle of a film. It was being raised every time the stream had finished filling its buffer, so a moment of catching up put it over the picture.
* Tapping the picture brings the controls up in a fifth of a second. It used to be a fixed third of a second of nothing, because the app waited out the whole double tap window before it would react at all. Measured at 305 ms before and 200 ms after, and the fades are quicker too.
* The controls slide in from the top and the bottom of the picture, and leave the same way, instead of simply appearing.
* A double tap never disturbs the controls, whether they are showing or not. Skipping ten seconds with the bar already up used to send it away and bring it back.
* The buttons in the middle of the player have a soft shadow, so they stay visible over a bright scene.
* A source that offers more than one audio language now says so in the source list.

* Subtitles are no longer retimed automatically. Measuring the soundtrack and shifting the subtitles to match now happens only when you ask for it, using Sync subtitles in the subtitle menu. It used to run by itself on every title, and a wrong correction is far more noticeable than a right one: subtitles that were already correct could be moved part way through a film. A switch in Settings turns the automatic behaviour back on for anyone who prefers it.
* Sync subtitles no longer reports success when it has not worked. It used to check its answer by repeating the same measurement on the same audio, which agrees with itself whether it is right or wrong. It now measures two separate stretches of the soundtrack, acts only when both agree, and says so plainly when they do not.
* Sync subtitles now runs in a small panel in the corner instead of covering the screen, so the film keeps playing while it works.
* Timing corrections remembered from earlier versions have been cleared. They were produced by the automatic correction that no longer runs, so none of them reflected a choice anybody made.
* Subtitles are now chosen once and then left alone. Several parts of the app could quietly replace a subtitle file or retime it while a title was playing, which is why subtitles could drift, or disappear and return on a different file, after seeking or changing a setting.
* Fixed subtitles that started out correct and grew steadily worse as a title went on. Timings were being rescaled using a frame rate taken from the subtitle listing, and that figure is frequently wrong. A perfectly timed subtitle could be twelve seconds out within five minutes and over two minutes out by the end, at which point it stopped appearing at all.
* Rebuilt how subtitle timing is measured against the soundtrack. Speech used to be identified as any loud audio, which on anything with a score underneath it means almost all of the runtime, and animated films were worst affected. Speech is now distinguished from music by its rhythm rather than its volume.
* Sync subtitles no longer makes you wait. It previously needed several minutes of a film to have played before it could answer, and a single skip reset the wait. It now reads the soundtrack ahead of playback and answers in about a minute from anywhere in a title.
* Drift is measured across the whole title rather than a single scene. A subtitle that slides slowly out of step is only a fraction of a second wrong across any few minutes, which is too small to detect reliably, so two points far apart are compared instead.
* Timing corrections are only applied at rates a subtitle can genuinely have. Files go out of step because they were prepared for a different frame rate, so corrections are matched against those known rates rather than stretching a title by an arbitrary figure.
* Subtitles are no longer moved on weak evidence. A marginal reading could previously shift a correct subtitle by several seconds.
* Subtitle position can be adjusted up or down the picture, from Settings and from the subtitle appearance menu.
* Fixed subtitles being lost when switching source. The app dropped the subtitle it had before looking for a replacement, so if none was found you were left with none at all. It now keeps what it has until something better arrives.
* Fixed two subtitles being drawn on top of one another after switching source, retrying, or returning from an advert.
* Fixed subtitles appearing in a language nobody asked for. Where a title had no subtitles in your language, the first available track was enabled instead, which is how a viewer set to Spanish could end up reading Estonian.
* Subtitle languages are now recognised however a source labels them. The same language is written several different ways, and a mismatch meant an available subtitle was treated as missing.
* Fixed the preferred subtitle language in Settings being ignored. The setting was saved but never read, so subtitles followed the interface language instead.
* Fixed subtitles arriving in the wrong language for some viewers, and translated subtitles being processed as though the original was always English.
* Fixed occasional wrong characters, usually an accented letter shown as a question mark, in otherwise correct subtitles.
